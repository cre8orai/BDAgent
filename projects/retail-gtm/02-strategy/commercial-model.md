# Commercial model

This file frames the decisions; it does not yet make them.

## The margin stack

Every deal in this channel splits value across three or four parties. Write the stack
explicitly before negotiating anything:

```
  consumer price
    − retail / platform take         (if the partner sells through a third party)
    − partner margin                 (the intermediary's reason to do this at all)
    − licensing royalty              (segments A and C — often 10–20% [ASSUMPTION, see B3])
    − Cre8or COGS + margin
    = the room available
```

The binding question: **after a licensing royalty and a partner margin, is there enough
room left for Cre8or's on-demand cost structure to work at low volumes?** Small runs cost
more per unit. If the stack only closes at high volume, the whole "start at 12" pitch is
a demo feature rather than a business model, and the strategy needs to change.

`[OPEN]` A1 answers this. It is the single most important unknown in the project.

## Models to choose between

| Model | Cre8or gets | Partner gets | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wholesale / reseller** | Fixed price per unit | Whatever margin they set | Segments A, E, F |
| **Revenue share** | % of net sales | Lower cash outlay, shared risk | Segment C, unproven launches |
| **Cost-plus + platform fee** | COGS + margin + SaaS seat | Transparency, control | Segments B, D |
| **Referral** | Full customer relationship | Introduction fee | Tier 3, and any conflicted account |

`[OPEN]` Likely not one model. Probable answer: wholesale for Tier 2 volume, bespoke
rev-share for the first marquee programme to de-risk it for both sides.

## Decisions required

`[OPEN]` **Who funds the first run?** Cre8or's story is that nobody carries inventory —
but production is paid for before sell-through. Deposit? Partner PO? Cre8or on the hook?
This determines how much working capital the channel consumes as it scales, and it is
the question a CFO will ask first.

`[OPEN]` **Whose brand is on the box?** White-label builds partner loyalty and hides
Cre8or. Co-branding builds Cre8or equity and may reduce partner willingness to resell.
`[ASSUMPTION]` Licensors will refuse co-branding on licensed IP — verify in E4.

`[OPEN]` **Minimums.** Does a partner commit to annual volume in exchange for pricing?
Tension: minimums contradict the "no inventory risk" pitch, but without them the channel
is unforecastable.

`[OPEN]` **Exclusivity.** Category, territory, or client exclusivity will be requested by
any Tier 1 partner. Decide the default answer, and the price of a yes, before it's asked.

`[OPEN]` **Channel conflict.** The rule for when a partner's client is also a direct
Cre8or prospect. Needed *before* the first partner agreement, not after the first dispute.

`[OPEN]` **Payment terms and FX.** Partners of this size will push 60–90 day terms.
Model the cash conversion cycle against production payment timing.
