# Positioning — what we say to a channel partner

**Status:** ⚪ Draft frame. Do not finalise before `01-research/` A1–A3 and B4 land —
the argument below is only as good as the real numbers behind it.

## The one-sentence position

> Cre8or is the beauty supply chain for organisations that already own an audience but
> can't manufacture for it — turning a product line from an inventory bet into a
> variable cost.

## The argument, in the order it should be made

1. **Name their risk before naming our product.** Event-driven and audience-driven
   product lines fail on inventory, not on demand. Guess high, eat the surplus; guess
   low, miss the peak. Every partner in the model has lived this.
2. **Show why beauty specifically is blocked for them.** Conventional cosmetic
   manufacturing forces the guess: high MOQs, long lead times, tooling and NRE costs
   amortised over volume you must commit to before you have a single data point.
   `[OPEN]` needs B4's real comparison numbers to be credible.
3. **Then the capability.** 12 units to 50,000, same supplier, same SKU, volume driven
   by live POS data. This is the only claim that matters and it should be stated plainly.
4. **Then the rest of the stack.** Formulation, packaging, compliance, fulfilment —
   one vendor, not four. This is the reason to *keep* using us, not the reason to start.
5. **Then their economics.** What the partner makes per line, and how many lines a year
   becomes possible when the cost of a failed launch collapses. `[OPEN]` needs A1.

## What not to lead with

- The AI. Every vendor in every category claims AI in 2026. It is a *reason to believe*
  the demand-planning claim, not the claim itself.
- The technology stack, the dashboard, the platform architecture. Partners buy an
  outcome; the OS is how we deliver it, not what they're purchasing.
- Cre8or's manufacturing heritage as an opener. It is powerful *evidence* and weak
  *positioning* — it answers "can you actually do this?", which is question two, not one.

## Proof we need and don't yet have

`[OPEN]` A reference programme — one partner-led line, with real sell-through numbers.
Until that exists, every pitch is an argument rather than a demonstration. Getting the
first one, at almost any commercial terms, is worth more than the margin on it.
