# Partner tiers and prioritisation

**Status:** ⚪ Scoring framework only — segments deliberately unscored until research lands.

## Scoring

Score each candidate 1–5 on five axes. Weights are a starting proposal; adjust once A1
(unit economics) tells us where Cre8or actually makes money.

| Axis | Weight | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Volume potential | 30% | Lines per year × units per line |
| Winnability | 25% | How long and how gated is the cycle? |
| Strategic value | 20% | Does this logo open the next three doors? |
| Operational fit | 15% | Can we service their SKU mix, geography, speed? |
| Brand risk | 10% | What happens to Cre8or if their line fails publicly? |

## Proposed tier definitions

- **Tier 1 — Strategic.** Named-account pursuit, bespoke commercials, exec sponsorship,
  co-investment in the first programme. Few of these; each gets a plan of its own.
- **Tier 2 — Managed.** Standard partner agreement, defined margin, onboarding and
  enablement. The volume of the channel lives here.
- **Tier 3 — Referral.** No reselling; they introduce, we close, they take a fee.
  Existing referral/finder's agreement templates likely cover this — see research A7.

## The sequencing question

`[OPEN]` The real decision is whether to chase a marquee Segment A logo first (slow,
high-risk, but every subsequent conversation gets easier) or prove the operating model
on a fast Segment F or B deal first (low prestige, but produces the reference programme
that positioning says we critically lack).

Argument for proving first: a Segment A licensor will ask "who else have you done this
for?" on the first call, and there is currently no answer. Getting an answer is cheap
in Segment F and expensive in Segment A.

Decide once A2 (lead times) is known — if concept-to-shipment is genuinely fast, a proof
deal could land inside the sales cycle of a marquee pursuit, and the choice dissolves.
