# Charter — Retail / indirect GTM

## Why now

The direct motion (Cre8or → brand owner) scales linearly with sales headcount. Every
deal needs the same education, the same trust-building, the same onboarding. An
indirect motion scales differently: one agency or licensor relationship can generate a
recurring stream of product lines, each one a new manufacturing programme, without a
new trust-building cycle.

The capability that makes Cre8or worth reselling — start at 12 units, scale to 50,000,
driven by live POS data — is *most* valuable to organisations that launch many
short-life, audience-driven product lines. That is precisely what agencies, licensors,
and merch operators do for a living.

## Objectives

1. Define who the intermediary segments are and rank them by value and winnability.
2. Establish the commercial model — margin stack, who holds inventory risk, who owns
   the brand and the customer, minimums, exclusivity.
3. Build a repeatable pitch that works on a partner's *economics*, not on Cre8or's
   feature list.
4. Produce a named target list and the outbound motion to work it.

## Success criteria

`[OPEN]` These need David's numbers before they mean anything. Proposed shape:

- N signed channel partners in the first 12 months
- N product lines launched *through* partners
- Revenue through indirect as a % of total
- Time from partner brief → first shipment (the number partners will actually judge us on)

## In scope

Agencies (creative, brand, e-commerce), licensing and merchandise aggregators, talent
and creator management, brand aggregators/roll-ups, retailer private-label programmes,
distributors and promotional-products channels.

## Out of scope

The direct-to-brand-owner motion, and the DTC platform product roadmap. This project
consumes Cre8or's existing capability; it does not redesign it.

## Key decisions still open

`[OPEN]` **Whose brand is on the box?** White-label (partner's client's brand, Cre8or
invisible) vs. co-branded ("powered by Cre8or"). This changes positioning, pricing, and
whether the channel builds Cre8or equity or spends it.

`[OPEN]` **Who carries inventory risk?** Cre8or's whole story is that nobody has to.
But someone funds the first run. Confirm how this actually works commercially today.

`[OPEN]` **Channel conflict.** What happens when a partner's client is also a direct
Cre8or prospect? Need a rule before the first partner asks.

`[OPEN]` **Compliance liability.** MoCRA and international cosmetic regs — who is the
responsible person when the product ships under a third party's brand? This is a real
blocker for licensors and they will ask on the first call.

`[OPEN]` **Do partners get platform access?** Does an agency get a Cre8orOS seat to
manage their clients' lines, or do they transact through Cre8or's team? A partner-facing
dashboard is a moat; it's also a product commitment.

## Assumptions to validate

`[ASSUMPTION]` Intermediaries are currently blocked from offering beauty lines because
conventional cosmetic MOQs and lead times make small/uncertain runs uneconomic.
`[ASSUMPTION]` Beauty carries higher margin than the apparel/hard-goods merch these
partners default to, making it an attractive category extension.
`[ASSUMPTION]` These partners are willing to be a reseller rather than demanding a
referral-fee-only arrangement. (Note: referral/finder's agreement templates already exist
in the Cre8or document set — worth checking what's been used before.)
