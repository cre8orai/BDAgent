# The six investors with a funded manufacturing thesis

`[status 2026-09-08]` The most important research finding of the fundraise. Almost every
beauty investor is a **brand-equity buyer with no manufacturing precedent** — they buy
brands and will need Cre8or's story reframed before it is legible. These six have already
written a cheque for this shape of company.

Spend the outreach here first.

## 1. VMG Partners — best overall thesis match
$10–200M consumer / $4–40M technology · $1B Fund VI · San Francisco

They have already funded all three pieces of Cre8or, separately:
- **Specright** — AI platform for formulas, ingredients, packaging and BOMs, serving beauty
- **Good Face Project** — cloud cosmetics R&D lab, the closest live analogue to Cre8orOS
- **Afresh** — AI replacing forecast-driven ordering with live-data ordering. Structurally
  Cre8or's exact argument, in grocery instead of beauty.

VMG Technology explicitly backs founders solving problems in consumer, retail **and
manufacturing**. Beauty book: Kosas, Nécessaire, Snif, Danessa Myricks; exited Drunk
Elephant, K18, Briogeo.

**Contacts:** Robin Tsai (Managing Partner, sits across both consumer and technology — the
exact seam Cre8or occupies), Carle Stenmark (GP, Technology — for the OS story).

## 2. Monogram Capital Partners — owns a beauty CM and is rolling it up
$5M revenue floor · up to $50M+ · $350M Fund III · Beverly Hills

Prime Matter Labs acquired Cosmetic Development Laboratories (2021) and **Mana Products**
(Jan 2026, $100M+ revenue, manufactures for Rare Beauty and Fenty). Their own positioning:
unlike peers who invest only in consumer brands, they back "the supply chain and services
businesses that power them." Manufacturing is a named vertical.

`[RISK]` Prime Matter Labs is a direct competitor. They are both the best-informed audience
and a disclosure risk. Decide what stays out of the room before the first meeting.

**Contacts:** Oliver Nordlinger (Co-Founder, beauty lead), Jared Stein. Operating advisors
Tiffany Bensley and Christina Han cover beauty and personal care.

## 3. Gauge Capital — names contract manufacturing in its own marketing
$20–100M per platform · $5–40M EBITDA · ~$3B AUM · Southlake TX

The only firm found whose own materials list, under Food & Consumer: "Beauty Products,"
"Private Label & Contract Manufacturing," "Sustainable Packaging," "Functional Ingredients."
Cre8or reads as a Gauge platform thesis rather than a pitch.

**Contact:** Andrew Stanleick joined July 2026 as Managing Director and Operating Partner —
a senior beauty operator, and the right first call.

## 4. Knox Lane — owns and is consolidating beauty CDMO capacity
$3.5B AUM · San Francisco

Majority owner of **Elevation Labs** (premium beauty formulator/manufacturer, Idaho Falls
and Denver, 100+ brands across 80+ categories), which then acquired Boomerang Laboratories
(2024). They understand fill-line economics and MOQ rigidity from the inside.

**Contacts:** John Bailey (Managing Partner, public voice on the Elevation deal), David
Coghlan (Principal). Tarang Amin, CEO of e.l.f. Beauty, is on the advisor bench.

## 5. Willow Growth Partners — best early-stage fit, cheque matches this round
$2–4M initial, **leads** · targets $2–5M TTM revenue · Malibu

Runs an explicit software filter on its portfolio page and holds **Daash** (predictive
commerce intelligence using AI for retail brands, primarily beauty), **Ox** (supply-chain
and retail automation) and **FlavorCloud** (AI logistics) alongside Kosas, Bubble, Dae and
True Botanicals. AI + supply chain + beauty already coexist in one portfolio, and Daash is a
live comparable for the POS-to-production narrative.

**Contacts:** Deborah Benton (Founder & Managing Partner), Amanda Schutzbank (Co-Founder & GP).

## 6. Yellow Wood Partners — carve-outs with no captive plant
Consumer only · $750M Fund III · Boston

Their model is buying orphaned brands out of Unilever, Haleon, Bayer and Nestlé and standing
them up on **independent supply chains** — six carve-outs from five global consumer companies.
Evermark is a ~$1.9B platform (Suave, St. Ives, Pond's, TIGI). The operating bench tells the
story: two dedicated supply-chain partners plus product/package development and operations.

The pitch is the problem they face daily: a newly carved-out brand with no captive plant and
long-tail SKUs no large filler will run at 108 units.

## The reframe for everyone else

Outside these six, the pitch that travels is **"we are the infrastructure your portfolio
brands already need"** — Cre8orOS as the margin and software story, with the factory as proof
rather than as the ask.
